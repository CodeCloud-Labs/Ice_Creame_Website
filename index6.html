Our Philosophy

Spaces Designed to
Make You Feel at Home

Every corner of Velvet Scoop was designed with one belief — that the surroundings you eat in are as important as what you eat. Our interiors are not a backdrop. They are part of the experience itself.

We worked with local craftsmen, a Lucknow-based lighting studio, and an acoustic engineer to create spaces that look beautiful, feel comfortable, and sound just right.

Artisan Materials Throughout
Hand-laid terrazzo, lime-plaster walls, reclaimed teak accents and Italian velvet — sourced thoughtfully, installed carefully.
Circadian Lighting Design
Our lighting studio programmed warm-to-cool transitions across the day so every space always feels exactly right for the hour.
Live Botanicals Maintained Weekly
Over 200 live plants tended by a resident botanist — they clean the air and lift the spirit.

01
Material Detail

Italian Velvet
Upholstery

Every booth seat and banquette is upholstered in Italian velvet from a mill in Bergamo. We chose three colourways — deep burgundy, forest green, and midnight amber — to give each zone its own personality within a unified palette. The fabric is heavy, warm to the touch, and ageless in the way good materials always are.

Bergamo, Italy Three colourways Stain-treated
02
Material Detail

Hand-Laid
Terrazzo Floors

Our floors were laid over six weeks by a team from Jaipur who specialise in traditional terrazzo. The mix was customised with fine gold-fleck aggregate so that in the right light the floor itself seems to glow. No two panels are the same — each one a unique composition of stone chips, marble fragments and resin, sealed and polished for three days.

Jaipur artisans Gold aggregate Six weeks to lay
03
Material Detail

Lime Plaster
Archwork

Each arch was finished by hand with tadelakt lime plaster in three layered pigments — a technique borrowed from traditional Lucknowi haveli architecture. Two days per arch: the first coat sets the base tone, the second adds depth, and the third is burnished with a stone to produce the faint sheen you notice only when light catches the wall at an angle.

Tadelakt technique Lucknow heritage craft Three-coat burnishing
